Step 0

"Are we solving the right problem?"

Before root-cause analysis. Before interventions. Before resources are committed. Step Zero challenges the assumption that the problem as presented is the problem that needs solving.

This instrument moves you through four validation phases — Identify, Validate, Contextualize, and Reframe — producing a Validated Problem Charter that tells you what to do next.

Estimated time: 15–20 minutes.

Phase 01 · Identify

Document the Problem Claim

Capture the problem exactly as it was reported to you — before any analysis or reframing. The goal here is precision, not interpretation. What was handed to you?

Why this matters
The way a problem is stated often reflects the beliefs, biases, and blind spots of the person reporting it. Capturing it verbatim before you analyze it is the first act of discipline in Step Zero.
Phase 02 · Validate

Examine Objective Evidence

Now push back on the claim. What do you actually know versus what you are assuming? Evidence is what changes the direction of an investigation. Opinion is what reinforces the direction already set.

Common trap
Organizations often skip this phase and proceed directly to solutions. The result is a well-executed answer to the wrong question.
Not at all confident Fully confident
Phase 03 · Contextualize

Identify Affected People and Systems

Problems do not exist in isolation. This phase maps the human, operational, and systemic context surrounding the problem — because the solution must fit the system, not just the symptom.

Simple and contained Highly complex
Phase 04 · Reframe

Distinguish Symptom from Problem

This is the governing act of Step Zero. Having gathered evidence and mapped context, you now determine whether the problem as reported is the problem that needs solving — or whether it is a symptom of something deeper.

The critical distinction
A symptom is what is visible. The problem is what is causing it. Treating a symptom without addressing the problem produces temporary relief and recurring gaps.
Proceed
The problem is validated, accurately scoped, and worth solving. Root-cause analysis and intervention planning can begin.
Reframe Required
The problem exists but is not accurately defined. The problem statement must be revised before investigation continues.
Investigate Further
Insufficient evidence to validate or refute the claim. Additional data collection or stakeholder engagement is required before a decision can be made.
No Action Required
The evidence does not support the problem claim as stated. The issue does not warrant further investigation or intervention at this time.
